## Sath I: O Sigi Ódhinnion
-# Chapter I: Of Sigi, Son of Odin

Sí iesta e·narn a threnar o dîr i estar Sigi, i ve nern in·waith nî iond nan Ódhinn. O dîr egel aech drenar e·narn, i estannen Skadhi. Te veleg a churweb, ach hui abaethanner gwaith e·lûf han nî Sigi e·veleg vigdin a rhodonnen thar Skadhi.
-# Now begins the tale and tells of a man that was called Sigi, who according to the tales of the people was a son of Odin. Of another man also tells the tale, called Skaði. He was mighty and skilled of hand, but Sigi was the mighty one among them and nobler-born than him, as spoke the people of that time.

Nî an Skadhi mûl i mboe ennad vin narn, i estannen Bredhi. Te acharaed vi thais i nistanner naden. Mi cherth ah e·guru dín nî vaer ve waith emmail tharden, a maer thar of.
-# Skaði had [=to Sk. was] a slave that one must name [~mention] in the tale, called Breði. He was very effective in the labours that were thrust to him. In his deeds and skill [he] was good as people worthier than him, and better than some.

Si mboe narad i nî i elidh Sigi an farad erais a guden e·vûl han; a farasser drin aur bân ten thîn. Io en·aduial han ti govanner in·erais in annanger, ah elo, daer a dae-rem farn Bredhi thar farn Sigi, a Sigi dae edif han. Te ebent i 'aeda den i obul mûl raudad athar den mi faras in·erais, ta annur den ah annanc a chaudhant vil loss.
-# Now it must be told that it once was that Sigi fared to hunt deer and that slave [was] with him; and they hunted through the whole day till the evening. When that evening [lit. early-twilight] they gathered the deer that they had slain, behold, far more and larger was Breði's prey than Sigi's, and Sigi disliked that very much. He said that it astounds him that a slave could excel beyond him in the hunting of deer, so he attacked and killed and buried him in the snow.

e·Dinnu han te ndanodul na mbardh a phent i Vredhi wanorthas othen n’ en·eryn thraw. “Rong líthas te onin,” ebent, “a lhaw-iston oh allad am othen.” 
-# Before dusk [lit. late-twilight] he returned home and said that Breði rode away from him into the wild woods. “Soon he was lost from me,” he said, “and I knew nothing more of him.”

Skadhi lo-hederiant narn Sigi ah ammodh i gauth dhaun vi phith dín ah i de annanc Vredhi. Ta Skadhi vennant vŷr nodui añ gestad den a thi echírer den vi chaudh loss of. To amarthant Skadhi i doler estad e·chaudh han Haudh Bredhi, a chad han in·waith ui agórer dal, ah î estanner dal il choedh loss dhaer. Si enif danc i annanc Sigi e·vŷl a dollant e·ndaen dín. Eth mbarthanner den nad neithan vaudannen ve ñgaur vi said aer, ah anden baun gwaed em bar adared.
-# Skaði did not trust Sigi’s tale and believed that there was deception in his words and that he killed Breði. So Skaði sent some men to search for him and they found him in some mound of snow. Then Skaði declared that that mound shall be called “Breði’s Mound”, and after that the people ever did so, and indeed called thus every large mound of snow. Now it seemed certain that Sigi murdered the thrall and concealed his corpse. Therefore he was doomed to be an exile forbidden as a wolf in holy places, and he had to leave the house of his father.
